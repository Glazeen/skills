# Landing Page Blueprints

Starting structures by conversion goal. These are **starting points, not templates to fill in blindly**. Adjust section order and length to the visitor's awareness level and traffic source (see *Adjusting for awareness* below), and let the council challenge them. Each blueprint gives the section order, what each section must do, and goal-specific guidance on CTAs, forms, proof and common mistakes.

## Universal backbone

Most high-converting landing pages are variations of this arc. It combines Miller's SB7 order with the classic direct-response flow:

1. **Hero**: who it's for, the outcome, the primary CTA, and a proof snippet
2. **Problem / stakes**: the visitor's pain in their own words (VOC), and the cost of inaction
3. **Solution / mechanism**: what it is and *why it works differently* (Schwartz: the mechanism matters most in sophisticated markets)
4. **How it works**: a 3-step plan (Miller), which reduces perceived effort and risk
5. **Benefits / features**: features translated into outcomes
6. **Proof**: testimonials, logos, numbers, case studies (Cialdini: from similar people, placed near the decisions)
7. **Offer**: exactly what they get, the price or "free," and the risk reversal
8. **Objections / FAQ**: the real top 5 objections
9. **Final CTA**: restate the outcome, plus CTA and microcopy

Keep the **attention ratio** close to 1:1 on campaign pages: no global nav, a minimal footer (legal links only).

## Adjusting for awareness and traffic

| Visitor awareness (Schwartz) | Typical traffic | Page approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Most aware** | Retargeting, existing customers, branded search | Short. Lead with the offer, price or deal, and the CTA. Minimal persuasion |
| **Product aware** | Comparison searches, competitor terms, review sites | Lead with differentiation and proof. Comparison table, guarantees |
| **Solution aware** | Category searches ("best CRM for…"), content readers | Lead with the outcome and mechanism. Show why this approach beats alternatives |
| **Problem aware** | Problem searches, social ads targeted by pain | Lead with the problem in their words, then introduce the solution category and the product. Longer |
| **Unaware** | Cold social, display, broad audiences | Lead with a story, a surprising fact or an identity hook. Longest. Often better to offer a low-friction lead magnet than a hard ask |

**Message match:** whatever the traffic source promised (ad headline, email subject, keyword), the hero headline repeats or clearly continues it. One page per major promise or segment beats one generic page.

## 1. Lead generation
*For B2B services, agencies, consultants, high-ticket offers, or a lead magnet download. Goal: a qualified contact.*

| # | Section | Must do | Notes |
|---|---------|---------|-------|
| 1 | **Hero** | Outcome headline for a named audience; subheadline with what you get; form or CTA above the fold | Lead magnet: show a mockup of the asset. Consultation: show the person or team |
| 2 | **Problem** | 2–4 pains in VOC language | Bullets or a short paragraph |
| 3 | **What you get** | Specific contents of the lead magnet or consultation ("12-point audit of your…") | Specific deliverables raise perceived value |
| 4 | **Who it's for / not for** | Self-selection (Godin) | Improves lead quality |
| 5 | **How it works** | 3 steps from submit to outcome | Sets expectations, reduces anxiety |
| 6 | **Proof** | Results from similar clients, logos, credentials of the expert | Put a testimonial right next to the form |
| 7 | **About the guide** | Empathy plus authority (Miller): why this team, briefly | Not a company history |
| 8 | **FAQ** | Cost, time commitment, "what happens after I submit," privacy | |
| 9 | **Final CTA** | Repeat the offer, form or button, and microcopy | |

- **Form:** lead magnet = email (plus first name if personalizing). Consultation = name, email, company, one qualifying question (size or budget range, challenge). Use multi-step if there are more than 4 fields.
- **CTA copy:** "Send me the checklist," "Get my free audit," "Book my strategy call."
- **After submit:** instant delivery, plus a thank-you page with the next step (book a call, or a related resource) and a nurture sequence.
- **Common mistakes:** a generic "Contact us" form, no stated deliverable, no qualification (sales flooded with poor-fit leads), slow follow-up.

## 2. SaaS free trial or demo
*Goal: a trial signup (product-led) or a demo booking (sales-led).*

| # | Section | Must do | Notes |
|---|---------|---------|-------|
| 1 | **Hero** | Outcome headline; subheadline (what + for whom + how); primary CTA; product visual | Real UI screenshot or short loop, not abstract art. "No credit card required" microcopy for trials |
| 2 | **Social proof strip** | Logos of recognizable customers or ratings (G2, Capterra) | Directly under the hero |
| 3 | **Problem / old way** | The status quo pain and its cost | Name the real alternative (spreadsheets, a legacy tool, an agency) |
| 4 | **New way / mechanism** | What's different about this approach | The core differentiator |
| 5 | **Key benefits (3–4)** | Each benefit plus a product screenshot showing it | Alternate left and right; annotate screenshots |
| 6 | **How it works / time to value** | Setup steps, integrations | Reduces switching-cost objection |
| 7 | **Proof** | Case study with numbers, testimonials by role | Match the roles to the target buyer |
| 8 | **Comparison** (optional) | Versus the main alternative | For product-aware traffic |
| 9 | **Pricing / plans** or pricing link | Clarity on cost and the free tier or trial terms | Hidden pricing hurts self-serve |
| 10 | **Security / compliance** | SOC 2, GDPR, SSO | Essential for B2B mid-market and up |
| 11 | **FAQ** | Trial terms, migration, contracts, support | |
| 12 | **Final CTA** | | |

- **Form:** trial = email (or SSO with Google or Microsoft) and password; ask other details in onboarding. Demo = name, work email, company size, plus an optional "what do you want to see," with instant calendar booking after submit (scheduling embed).
- **CTA copy:** "Start free trial," "Start my 14-day trial," "Get a demo," "See it in action." Avoid "Sign up" with no value.
- **Consider:** an interactive demo or product tour as a transitional CTA (Gardner's try-before-you-buy; Miller's transitional CTA).
- **Common mistakes:** a feature-dump hero, an abstract illustration instead of the product, the demo form going to a black hole, a pricing surprise.

## 3. E-commerce product landing page
*Goal: add to cart or purchase. Often a single hero product from paid social.*

| # | Section | Must do | Notes |
|---|---------|---------|-------|
| 1 | **Hero** | Product name plus outcome or benefit headline; price; rating with review count; add-to-cart; image gallery | Gallery: product in use, scale, details. Price visible |
| 2 | **Key benefits** | 3–5 icon bullets (outcome-first) | Scannable |
| 3 | **Offer and risk reversal** | Shipping, returns, guarantee, delivery estimate | Near the add-to-cart button |
| 4 | **Problem → solution story** | Why this product exists, and the before and after | Longer for unaware or cold social traffic |
| 5 | **How it works / how to use** | Steps, video | |
| 6 | **Social proof** | Reviews with photos, UGC, press mentions | Filterable reviews; show the negatives handled honestly |
| 7 | **Comparison** | vs. alternatives or other versions | |
| 8 | **Specs / ingredients / materials** | Details for careful buyers | Accordion |
| 9 | **FAQ** | Sizing, shipping, returns, care | |
| 10 | **Final CTA plus bundle or upsell** | | |

- **Mobile first:** a sticky add-to-cart bar, an image gallery that works with swipes, wallet payments (Apple Pay, Google Pay, Shop Pay) to reduce checkout friction.
- **Urgency:** only real stock levels or real sale end dates (Cialdini's ethics; consumer-protection law).
- **Common mistakes:** shipping cost revealed only at checkout, low-quality images, reviews hidden, a slow-loading gallery hurting LCP.

## 4. Webinar / event registration
*Goal: registration, and then attendance (track both).*

| # | Section | Must do | Notes |
|---|---------|---------|-------|
| 1 | **Hero** | Outcome-driven title (what they'll be able to do after); date, time, timezone, duration; register CTA | Auto-convert to the visitor's timezone if possible. A countdown is fine because the deadline is real |
| 2 | **What you'll learn** | 3–5 specific takeaways | Specific > vague ("the 3-email sequence that…") |
| 3 | **Who it's for** | Self-selection | |
| 4 | **Speakers** | Photo, credentials, why they're worth an hour | Authority (Cialdini) |
| 5 | **Proof** | Past attendee quotes, attendee count, notable companies attending | |
| 6 | **Agenda** (optional) | Time-blocked agenda for longer events | |
| 7 | **Bonus / incentive** | Replay, slides, template for attendees | Boosts attendance |
| 8 | **FAQ** | "Will there be a replay?", cost, platform | |
| 9 | **Final CTA** | | |

- **Form:** name and email (plus company or role for B2B lead scoring). Keep it short.
- **After submit:** add-to-calendar links, confirmation email, reminders (e.g., 24h, 1h, starting now), and a replay for no-shows.
- **Common mistakes:** a vague topic-only title ("Q3 Marketing Trends"), a missing timezone, no reminder sequence (registration without attendance).

## 5. Local service business
*Goal: a call, a quote request, or a booking (plumbers, dentists, clinics, salons, contractors, law firms).*

| # | Section | Must do | Notes |
|---|---------|---------|-------|
| 1 | **Hero** | Service + location headline ("Emergency plumber in [City], at your door in 60 minutes"); click-to-call button plus a quote or booking CTA; rating | Phone number visible and tappable on mobile |
| 2 | **Trust bar** | Google rating with review count, years in business, licenses, insurance | Local trust drives the decision |
| 3 | **Services** | List with brief outcomes, and prices or "from" prices if possible | |
| 4 | **Why us** | Differentiators: speed, guarantee, availability, specialization | Avoid "quality service" clichés |
| 5 | **How it works** | Call or request → visit or quote → done | |
| 6 | **Reviews** | Real Google or Yelp reviews with names, local references | |
| 7 | **Team / owner** | Real photos, names | Faces build trust (liking and authority) |
| 8 | **Service area** | Map or neighborhoods list | Also local SEO |
| 9 | **FAQ** | Pricing, availability, emergency, insurance and payment | |
| 10 | **Final CTA** | Call, form, or booking widget | |

- **Form:** name, phone, service needed, preferred time (plus an optional photo upload for quotes).
- **Plus:** `LocalBusiness` structured data, a consistent name, address and phone (NAP) with the Google Business Profile, opening hours visible, call tracking for attribution.
- **Common mistakes:** a phone number as an image (not tappable), a stock-photo team, no prices or ranges at all, a slow WordPress theme on mobile.

## 6. Pre-launch / waitlist
*Goal: waitlist signups and validation before the product exists.*

1. **Hero**: who it's for, the outcome or the new mechanism, an email field, and a launch timeframe if honest
2. **The problem**: in their words
3. **What's coming**: a concept visual or mockup (labeled as a preview), 3 key benefits
4. **Why join now**: genuine early-access perks (founding price, priority access, input on the roadmap)
5. **Founder story / guide**: why the team is building this
6. **FAQ**: when, price expectations, privacy
7. **Final CTA**, with an optional referral mechanic (move up the waitlist by sharing)

Godin's lens is especially relevant here: a small group that genuinely wants this beats a big list of the mildly curious. Add one qualifying question to learn who's signing up.

## Blueprint output format

When delivering a new page, use this format for each section so it can go straight to design or code:

```
### Section [N]: [Name]
**Purpose:** [what this section must accomplish for the visitor]
**Layout:** [e.g., two columns: copy left, product screenshot right; mobile stacks copy first]
**Copy:**
- Eyebrow: …
- Headline: …
- Body: …
- Bullets: …
- CTA: [button text] · Microcopy: […]
**Proof element:** [which testimonial/logo/stat and where]
**Advisor note:** [which lens shaped this section, and any open trade-off]
```

Then draft every section's copy in full. Mark any placeholder that needs real data (testimonials, numbers, logos) as `[NEEDS: real customer quote]`, and never invent testimonials or statistics.
